How the calculator works

Your target to date

Your mileage allowance is spread evenly over the calendar days of the term. With 30,000 mi over 36 months, you’re “allowed” around 10,000 mi once a third of the term has passed. If you’re above that, you’re driving more than your contract allows for.

The term ends the day before the same day of the month it started: a 36-month contract starting on 1 March 2025 ends on 29 February 2028.

The forecast

From your daily average so far, the calculator works out where you’ll end up at the end of the contract. It needs at least 14 days of the term for that – any earlier and the figure would be pure chance. If you want a figure sooner, or you know your driving is going to change, enter your own annual estimate.

With a single odometer reading, the calculator only knows your average since the start of the contract. The app keeps every reading and also works with the average of recent weeks, which shows sooner when you start driving more or less than before.

Tolerance and settlement

Nothing within the tolerance is charged or refunded. Beyond it, some contracts count only the mileage above the tolerance, others count all of it from the start – check which rule yours uses. Not every contract refunds unused mileage, and some that do cap the refund. You can set both under “More Contract Details”. If your contract states the excess mileage rate excluding VAT and VAT is added to the invoice, enter the rate including VAT.

Miles or kilometres, pounds or euros

The calculator doesn’t convert anything. Enter the figures as they appear in your contract and choose the matching currency and distance above the form – the result is shown in the same units.
